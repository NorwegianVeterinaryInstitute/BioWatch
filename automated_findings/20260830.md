🚀 Section A: Command-Line & Secure Analytical Tools (Newest First)

- **ResLit** (Release Date: 2026-08-21) #amr #python
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/Georgakopoulos-Soares-lab/ResLit) | [Web](https://www.reslit.info) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.14.744991)
    - Function: Skoulakis, Xiao, Provatas, Galaras, Pavlopoulos, Georgakopoulos-Soares (Georgakopoulos-Soares lab, UT Austin / Penn State) built an automated literature-mining pipeline and public database for AMR: BioMistral-7B screens PubMed abstracts (2M candidates down to 356,000 relevant), then Qwen3-30B extracts resistance genes, mutations, organisms and mechanisms from 117,000 full texts, cross-linked to CARD, ResFinder and the NCBI Reference Gene Catalog.
    - Use Case: Gives any AMR surveillance programme a continuously-growing, evidence-graded (four-tier), searchable alternative to manually tracking the AMR literature — 3,120 genes and 13,593 mutations so far, filterable by antibiotic/organism/country/year with CSV export, plus a curator-login workflow for expert validation of the automated extractions.
    - Usability Note: MIT license (code) / CC-BY 4.0 (data), comprehensive README, 129 commits — a reproducible pipeline (raw LLM outputs and manual audit annotations are archived alongside the code) rather than a black-box database; too new (0 stars) to assess issue-responsiveness.

ℹ️ Section B: Informational & Proprietary

- **Host breadth, genomic exchange and antimicrobial-resistance evolution in East African Campylobacter** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.24.746677)) — population-genomic study, no software release #amr #one-health
    - Function: Bahati, Mwakalapa, Mung'ong'o, Makaranga, Maghembe (AfroBiomics, Tanzania) analyse 722 public *C. jejuni*/*C. coli* genomes from Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda and find that animal-host breadth is *not* explained by simple lineage-wide measures of recombination, accessory-genome fluidity or AMR burden — host breadth instead reflects lineage-specific combinations of ecological opportunity and population history, with recurrent AMR evolution (tet(O), gyrA T86I) confined to a small set of determinants. A caution against assuming genome-exchange rate predicts host range for this EFSA-priority zoonotic pathogen in under-sampled regions.
- **Predicting the Risk of Avian Influenza Zoonosis using Viral Genome Sequencing Data** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.746166)) — ML classifier study, no confirmed new standalone code release [SURVEILLANCE-READY] #one-health
    - Function: Fairweather, Andrews, Grier, Brierley, Cattarino, Panovska-Griffiths (University of Oxford) train XGBoost classifiers on genetic-composition features (dipeptide composition, amino-acid physiochemistry) of all 8 AIV proteins to flag avian influenza genomes as zoonotic-risk or not, finding PB2 and HA features most informative and a multi-protein ensemble outperforming any single-protein model. Builds on the same group's earlier AIV-zoonosis classifier (bioRxiv 2025, code at [lbrierley/ai_for_ai](https://github.com/lbrierley/ai_for_ai)) — that repository is for the *predecessor* paper, not a confirmed standalone release for this specific extension, so it stays in Section B pending a dedicated code release.
- **Emerging Haemagglutinin Mutations in Bovine-origin H5N1 Influenza Viruses from Humans and Cattle Retain Avian Receptor Binding with Increased Stability** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.18.745528)) — experimental characterisation, no software release [SURVEILLANCE-READY] #one-health
    - Function: Yang, Peacock, Valdez, Zhou, Klim, Sukhova, Sadeyen, Brown, Barclay, Iqbal (The Pirbright Institute) test HA mutations from the ongoing US dairy-cattle H5N1 outbreak (>1,150 farms, 19 states by July 2026) and find all retain avian-type receptor preference, but two mutations (D88G, S94N) modestly raise HA pH-stability and thermostability versus wild type — still short of the stability range linked to efficient airborne transmission, but a concrete marker set for H5N1 (an explicit NVI/EFSA priority pathogen) genomic surveillance to watch for.
- **Evolutionary analysis supports variation in life history strategies between three foot-and-mouth-disease-virus serotypes** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.18.745431)) — phylodynamic analysis, no software release #one-health #evolution
    - Function: Holmes, Perez-Martin, Gubbins, Beechler, Jolles, Biek (University of Glasgow) analyse 716 published FMDV sequences across the three Southern African Territories serotypes and find SAT1 evolves slower (consistent with a more chronic-infection strategy) while SAT2 shows more variable evolutionary rates and some livestock-to-wildlife transmission signal — evidence that serotypes of this major multi-host livestock picornavirus differ in maintenance strategy, relevant to any wildlife/livestock interface FMD surveillance programme.
- **Genome-context-aware discovery of antibacterial peptides from bacterial small open reading frames** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.17.745349)) — methodology with wet-lab validation, no public code release #amr
    - Function: Li, Li (Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention) show that of 11,918 AMP-classifier-consensus candidate peptides mapped back to 649,653 bacterial genomes, 529 of 536 "consensus" candidates were artefacts of partial open reading frames near contig edges — only genome-context auditing separated genuinely recurrent, complete coding loci from classifier false positives, and a resulting 20-peptide panel showed low-micromolar antibacterial activity. A companion paper from the same group the same week (proteome-scale mining of *encrypted* AMPs embedded within larger proteins, [preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.18.745516)) reports a parallel pipeline with its own wet-lab-validated hits. Neither paper's code/data is public (both state "available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request"), so neither qualifies for Section A despite the validation work.
- **Serial acquisition of virulence determinants and aminoglycoside resistance by the emerging Streptococcus agalactiae sequence type 1010 lineage** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.17.745249)) — comparative genomics study, no software release #amr
    - Function: Farid, Haldeman, Otto, DMello, Tettelin, Ratner (NYU Grossman School of Medicine) use core-genome phylogenetics on 55 GBS ST1010 genomes (plus two new single-locus variants) to show this internationally-emerging lineage (US, Dominican Republic, Netherlands, Italy) carries virulence determinants (HvgA, Srr2) previously thought specific to the hypervirulent CC17 lineage, with high-level gentamicin resistance confined to one numerically dominant ST1010 subclade. A concrete argument for including ST1010 in routine GBS genomic surveillance alongside the established hypervirulent clones.
