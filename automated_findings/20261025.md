🚀 Section A: Command-Line & Secure Analytical Tools (Newest First)

- **RhizAeon** (Release Date: 2026-10-23) [EXPERIMENTAL] #evolution
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/veg/rhizaeon-code) | [bioRxiv preprint ("Clean Breaks")](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.19.760237)
    - Function: Kosakovsky Pond's group (Temple University Institute for Genomics and Evolutionary Medicine, with the HyPhy/datamonkey "veg" team) built a Rust/WebAssembly engine that detects homologous recombination and horizontal gene transfer by projecting aligned sequences into continuous metric-embedding coordinates and tracking trajectory breaks, instead of the usual choice between exponential-cost phylogenetic changepoint models or overcounting-prone sequence-triplet heuristics (3SEQ-style).
    - Use Case: Validated on sarbecovirus Spike chimeras, a 2.22 Mb multidrug-resistant *Streptococcus pneumoniae* chromosome, and 379 primate HLA Class I alleles — directly reusable for any lab trying to resolve recombination/HGT-driven mosaicism in bacterial AMR genomes or viral surveillance datasets in seconds rather than hours, including a browser-runnable WebAssembly build for interactive use without a local install.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, 117 commits, `pip install rhizaeon` CLI available — but brand new (0 stars, 1 fork, no issue history yet), so [EXPERIMENTAL] is on maturity grounds only, not validation quality; the benchmarks reported (false-positive rate 0.58-1.01% across >1,000,000 simulated nulls) are from the authors' own paper, not yet independently reproduced.

- **ARG-PASS v2.0** (Release Date: 2026-10-22) #amr #one-health
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/btroppo/ARG-PASS) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.21.760772)
    - Function: Haraoui lab (Université de Sherbrooke) updated their structure-based antibiotic-resistance-gene predictor, which flags ARGs by conserved protein sequence-structure signatures rather than raw homology, letting it recognise resistance enzymes with little sequence similarity to known references.
    - Use Case: Applied to a Kolkata lake metagenome and MGnify/PLSDB data, 10 of 11 candidate genes were functionally confirmed by expression in *E. coli*, and the tool predicted 918 divergent class B1 metallo-β-lactamases, tracing a chromosomal environmental reservoir reaching the human gut via fermented-food bacteria — a directly relevant approach for AMR surveillance work that needs to catch resistance genes current databases miss, not just re-detect known ones.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, comprehensive README (two operational modes, 24+ ARG classes documented), 57 commits — but 0 stars/forks and the v2.0 preprint is itself not yet peer-reviewed, so treat as an early-adoption research tool; it does build on a published v1 methodology and includes wet-lab functional validation, which is a stronger evidence base than most brand-new entries.

- **Panagram** (Release Date: 2026-10-22) #comparative-genomics #evolution
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/kjenike/panagram) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.21.760756)
    - Function: Schatz lab (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory / Johns Hopkins), with collaborators across several plant and animal genomics groups, built an alignment-free pangenome framework that quantifies shared/divergent sequence via pan-k-mer profiles instead of computationally expensive whole-genome multiple alignment.
    - Use Case: Identified gene-environment associations across 160 *Arabidopsis* genomes, introgressions in *Solanum* crop/wild-relative pangenomes, and breed-specific variation across a 51-haplotype *Canis* pangenome and 605 vertebrate genomes — a scalable option for any lab wanting to connect pangenome-scale structural variation to phenotype without an alignment bottleneck, including for structurally complex non-model genomes.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, 272 commits, 107 stars, 6 forks, 4 open issues (2 open PRs) — actively developed with real external uptake; issue-responsiveness specifically wasn't confirmable from the page content fetched this run.

- **Carbon-A (Carbon-Annotator)** (Release Date: 2026-10-21) #annotation #python
    - Links: [HuggingFace model card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceBio/Carbon-A-1.2B) | [Carbon GitHub](https://github.com/huggingface/carbon) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.20.760448)
    - Function: A Hugging Face / Zhongguancun Academy collaboration built a 1.2B-parameter encoder DNA foundation model that annotates protein-coding genes directly from raw genomic sequence with a single checkpoint across mammals, other vertebrates, invertebrates, plants, fungi and protozoa — replacing the usual taxon-specific ab initio annotators and the need for external RNA-seq/protein evidence.
    - Use Case: Used to build the Carbon Annotation Database (48,167 assemblies, 22,617 taxa, 566 million predicted genes — roughly 9x RefSeq's sequence coverage and 11x its taxon count), with PacBio Iso-Seq data from four species confirming novel gene calls absent from RefSeq — directly useful for annotating genomes of non-model parasites, pathogens or hosts that lack curated reference annotations.
    - Usability Note: Apache-2.0 license, open weights (confirmed on the HuggingFace model card), parent Carbon GitHub repo has 255 stars/39 forks/3 open issues and active maintenance — strong backing, though Carbon-A itself is a same-day sibling release not yet separately battle-tested outside the paper's own benchmarks.

💡 Methodological Trend

Three threads worth flagging this cycle, continuing watch-items from recent reports:

- **Agentic AI for scientific computing keeps escalating from assistive to autonomous discovery.** Two entries this cycle move the needle on the recurring agentic-AI thread (flagged across [[20260830]], [[20260913]], [[20260927]]): **"Autonomous AI agents discover reverse transcriptases with tandem repeat arrays"** (Anthropic, posted 2026-10-11) deployed unsupervised Claude Code instances to survey 1.9 billion protein clusters and surfaced a genuinely novel jumbo-phage reverse-transcriptase family with tandem-repeat arrays — the agents found it themselves by noticing atypical DNA repeats, not by following a prescribed pipeline. **"Distributed AI agents enable comprehensive discovery of biological insights at scale"** (Scripps/HHMI, posted 2026-10-16) scales this further, assigning many parallel agents to facets of a whole-brain Perturb-seq atlas; without seeing the source manuscript, the agents recovered its conclusions and were preferred by blinded human experts over single-agent analysis. Worth tracking as agentic analysis moves from "helps interpret" toward "makes the discovery."
- **AMR gene-calling-from-homology keeps getting caught overclaiming.** **"Most metagenomic vanT calls are not vancomycin resistance genes"** (HUN-REN Biological Research Centre, posted 2026-10-23) found only 3.7% of 107,008 metagenome-assembled genomes' predicted *vanTG* calls actually carry the required active-site substitution — the rest are misannotated alanine racemases. This directly echoes the GenoFlu/H5Nx genotyping caution flagged in [[20261109]], and pairs pointedly with this cycle's own **ARG-PASS v2.0** (Section A above), whose whole pitch is resistance-gene prediction that survives functional validation rather than stopping at homology — the field is visibly converging on "validate the call" as the next AMR-tooling bar, not just "find more candidates."
- **Genomic language models are starting to specialise for structural annotation, not just representation.** **GLARE** (DOE Joint Genome Institute, posted 2026-10-11, [code.jgi.doe.gov/NHaas/glare](https://code.jgi.doe.gov/NHaas/glare), BSD-variant license) fine-tunes a genomic language model (Nucleotide Transformer v3) specifically for transposable-element superfamily classification, beating existing classifiers on held-out taxonomic ranks and degraded sequence libraries. Taken with **Carbon-A** (Section A above), this cycle's genome-scale language models are increasingly built for one annotation task end-to-end (TE typing, gene-finding) rather than general-purpose embeddings a downstream model has to be trained on — a shift from [[20261109]]'s "bring your own ESM-2 embeddings" framing toward single-checkpoint annotation tools.

ℹ️ Section B: Informational & Proprietary

- **Population genomics of *Actinobacillus pleuropneumoniae*** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.19.759954), posted 2026-10-21) — genomic survey, no software release #variant-calling #one-health
    - Function: Iowa State's Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory analysed 328 genomes of this swine pleuropneumonia pathogen (153 newly sequenced), resolving 23 genomic lineages and showing that K:O capsular/LPS serotype combinations track lineage and Apx-toxin genotype far better than K-type alone — a useful reference framework for anyone doing WGS-based typing of this economically important livestock pathogen, directly relevant to food-security/swine surveillance work.
- **Bat-borne influenza A H9N2 species-specific replication** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.15.759517), posted 2026-10-16) — experimental host-range study, no software release #one-health #evolution
    - Function: A German/Egyptian consortium tested this pre-pandemic-flagged bat H9N2 virus (known for airborne ferret transmission) in two fruit-bat species plus pigs and quails: *Eidolon helvum* supported high-level replication and efficient transmission while *Rousettus aegyptiacus*, pigs and quails did not — narrowing the plausible reservoir/mixing-vessel host range for a virus already flagged as a pandemic-preparedness concern.
- **Antimicrobial resistance in a warmer world** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.16.759609), posted 2026-10-16) — experimental evolution study, no software release #amr
    - Function: University of Exeter show that in *E. coli*, kanamycin susceptibility and plasmid-conjugation thermal performance don't track bacterial growth-rate thermal performance the same way, so warming drives stronger AMR selection mainly via growth-rate/susceptibility effects rather than via increased horizontal transfer — a mechanistic piece of the broader climate-AMR link relevant to any lab tracking resistance trends under warming conditions.
- **Andes virus (MV Hondius outbreak) is highly pathogenic and airborne-shed in hamsters** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.12.758426), posted 2026-10-12) — outbreak phenotypic characterisation, no software release #emerging #one-health
    - Function: Erasmus MC/Rotterdam and collaborators characterised the Andes hantavirus strain from the May 2026 cruise-ship outbreak in golden Syrian hamsters, finding it highly pathogenic with airborne shedding detected in air samplers — in contrast to a rodent-derived reference isolate that barely shed — a notable finding for a virus whose person-to-person transmission potential is usually considered limited.
