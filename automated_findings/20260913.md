🚀 Section A: Command-Line & Secure Analytical Tools (Newest First)

- **PIGSTI** (Release Date: 2026-09-01) [SURVEILLANCE-READY] #one-health #metagenomics #snakemake
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/LouisLhote/PIGSTI) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.01.748539)
    - Function: L'Hôte, Butt, Halpin, Mattiangeli, Bangsgaard, Daly et al. (UCD Ruminant Palaeogenomics group, with an international ancient-DNA consortium) built a modular Snakemake pipeline that runs host identification, metagenomic screening and pathogen-authentication scoring in one pass over shotgun sequencing data from ancient animal remains.
    - Use Case: Screened 952 new and public animal palaeogenomic datasets and flagged roughly 15 ancient zoonotic/animal pathogens with high confidence, including the first documented ancient-animal cases of *Rickettsia felis* and *Leptospira borgpetersenii* — directly reusable by any lab mining museum or archaeological animal remains for historical zoonotic-pathogen presence, with output formatted for downstream population-genetics/phylogenetic analysis.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, clear README, 105 commits — actively developed; 0 stars and too new to assess issue-responsiveness.

- **StrainSpy** (Release Date: 2026-08-30) #metagenomics #r-lang
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/gtonkinhill/strainspy) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.30.748153)
    - Function: Mallawaarachchi, Tandon, Rajan, Marcelino, Sandhu, Bedoui, Ingle, Gunjur, Tonkin-Hill (Peter Doherty Institute / University of Melbourne) built an R package that runs strain-level microbiome-wide association testing directly on containment Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) profiles from Sylph, Sourmash or MetaPhlAn output, supporting longitudinal and multi-cohort designs.
    - Use Case: Applied to gut-microbiota recovery after antibiotic exposure and to a multi-cohort colorectal-cancer metagenome analysis, revealing novel strain-level associations, with containment-ANI-based ML models matching abundance-based methods for accuracy — gives any lab doing longitudinal or outbreak metagenomics a statistically principled alternative to abundance-only strain tracking.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, comprehensive README, 87 commits, 14 stars, 6 open issues — active development; too new to assess issue-responsiveness beyond the open-issue count.

- **antiGen** (Release Date: 2026-08-27) #evolution #emerging #python
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/evo-design/antiGen) | [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/antigen-model/) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.27.741102)
    - Function: Specht, Park, Chithrananda, Driscoll, Brixi, Palacios, Hie (Stanford University / Arc Institute Evo-design group) built antiGen, a machine-learning model that forecasts future viral mutations by learning directly from phylogenetic-tree structure rather than sequence alone, with transformer and ESM-2-based architectures plus baselines.
    - Use Case: State-of-the-art at predicting never-before-seen SARS-CoV-2 spike mutations — including ones emerging years after the training cutoff, with forecasted mutations retaining pseudoviral infectivity in vitro — and leads on influenza, RSV and dengue surface proteins despite far less training data; a directly reusable pandemic-preparedness tool for anticipating which mutations a tracked virus (including H5N1 HA) is likely to acquire next.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, detailed README covering data generation, training, inference and manuscript-figure reproduction, 109 commits, 10 stars — active development; too new to assess issue-responsiveness.

- **PhageTAILor** (Release Date: 2026-08-24) [EXPERIMENTAL] #comparative-genomics #evolution #python
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/hjcho-bio/PhageTAILor) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.24.746745)
    - Function: Cho, Hour, Roux, Coclet, Amusat, Mutalik, Kazakov, Levy, Nachmias, Aureli, Sweet, Visel, Ceballos, Basso (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory et al.) built a CLI pipeline combining six homology-based detectors (geNomad, tail-gene, PHROGs-tail, SecReT6, eCIS-tem, a divergence-tolerant tail-HMM) with a LightGBM classifier to find and classify phage tail-like elements — tailocins, bacterial T6SS, and eCIS contractile injection systems — in bacterial genomes.
    - Use Case: Hit F1 = 0.955 for tailocin detection on a 76-strain cross-clade benchmark, matching geNomad's sensitivity while avoiding its higher false-positive rate — gives any lab studying bacterial antagonism or contractile-nanomachine-mediated competition a single automated pipeline instead of running six detectors by hand and reconciling results manually.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, beginner-friendly single-command CLI with TSV/HTML output, 16 commits, 1 star — brand new; flagged [EXPERIMENTAL] on maturity grounds (too early to assess issue-responsiveness), not on validation quality.

💡 Methodological Trend

The recurring "agentic AI for scientific computing" thread (BacteReason/biomeStat/ChatGEM/MetaClaw, and the OpenAI field report, all flagged in [[20260816]]) gained a new entrant squarely aimed at this thread's own weak point — auditability. **From Prompt to Provenance: BloClaw, a Capability-Gated AI4S Workstation for Auditable Computational Biology** (Qin, Pang, Zhang — Beijing 1st Biotech Group, posted 2026-08-26) proposes an "AI4S workstation" where every agent capability declares its execution state, input constraints, dependencies and scientific limitations up front, with a provenance-aware "Living Lab Notebook" that records what actually executed versus what remains unvalidated, and routes failures to repair, retry or escalation rather than silently returning a plausible-looking answer. The manuscript is explicit that it reports system design and a narrow demonstration (RDKit-based molecular screening, a 6LU7 docking-pose inspection) plus a proposed evaluation protocol — not benchmark results yet — so this is a design contribution to watch rather than an adoptable tool today; no code repository was found for it this run.
