- **PathoBench** (Release Date: 2026-07-17) [EXPERIMENTAL] #emerging #cross-domain
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/BPHL-Molecular/pathobench) | [Web app](https://pathobench.vercel.app) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.16.739016)
    - Function: Dong, Li, Chiribau, Mitchell, Liu, Perkins (Florida Department of Health, Bureau of Public Health Laboratories) built an open, community-driven benchmark registry pairing 26 curated reference datasets across 10 human pathogens with pathogen-specific evaluation metrics, so submitted pipelines can only be compared head-to-head on the same dataset rather than on self-reported numbers.
    - Use Case: Gives public-health and clinical labs a shared, credibility-checked (ORCID-linked attribution, public peer comments, admin verification) way to pick a pathogen bioinformatics pipeline instead of trusting a tool's own paper — demonstrated by re-ranking four published *M. tuberculosis* drug-resistance pipelines against the WHO TB mutation catalogue and showing real discriminating power between them.

- **amR** (Release Date: 2026-07-13) #amr #r-lang
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/JRaviLab/amR) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.10.734579)
    - Function: Ghosh, Brenner, Boyer, McKim, Vang, Wolfe, Mayer, Lesiyon, Ravi (Ravi Lab) built a three-package R suite (amRdata, amRml, amRviz) that pulls paired genome/AST data from BV-BRC, builds pangenomes, extracts gene/domain/COG/ResFinder/structural-variant features, trains interpretable per-species-drug ML models, and ships an interactive Shiny dashboard for exploring results.
    - Use Case: End-to-end, reproducible AMR genotype-to-phenotype modelling that stays interpretable (feature importance per model) rather than a black-box call — demonstrated on *Shigella sonnei* across 23 drugs/drug classes (median MCC 0.89), directly reusable for building species- and drug-specific AMR predictors for any WGS'd bacterial pathogen with matching AST data.
    - Usability Note: BSD-3-Clause license, comprehensive README with workflow diagram, 16 commits, 3 stars, 2 forks — brand new, too early to assess issue-responsiveness. Installable via GitHub; stores data in Parquet/DuckDB for memory efficiency.

- **EDTA v2 (animal-genome extension)** (Release Date: 2026-07-06) #assembly #annotation
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/oushujun/EDTA) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.01.735963)
    - Function: Ou, Lu, Nguyen, Gerhardt, Fang, Rashid, Guhlin, Dainat, Bao, Bayer, Na, Benson formally report and benchmark EDTA's extension to direct LINE/SINE detection, a rewritten and faster TIR-Learner, and structural detectors accelerated up to two orders of magnitude, closing the non-LTR detection gap that had restricted EDTA mainly to plant genomes.
    - Use Case: Automated, standardized transposable-element annotation for animal genomes (tested on 30 Vertebrate Genomes Project genomes) — directly reusable for annotating repeat content in any newly assembled pathogen, host or vector genome where non-LTR retroelements (LINEs/SINEs) matter, not only plant genomes as before.
    - Usability Note: GPL-3.0, mature and heavily used repository (990 commits, 473 stars, 27 open issues with visible maintainer engagement). Flagged here as a validation/capability-extension paper, not a first release: EDTA's v2.x line has existed since 2022 (current tag v2.3.3, Aug 2026), and the specific LINE/SINE/TIR-Learner code described in this preprint could not be confirmed as merged into the public master branch from its most recent commit messages (Aug-Sep 2026) — worth confirming directly before relying on it for a live animal-genome annotation run.

- **SSUplex** (Release Date: 2026-07-05) #metagenomics #emerging
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/ayobi/ssuplex) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.02.736232)
    - Function: O'Brien, Vargas, Acuna, Parada (Universidad Andres Bello) built a Rust reimplementation of Metaxa2's SSU rRNA extraction-and-origin-sorting logic, detecting and classifying reads into bacterial/archaeal/eukaryotic/mitochondrial/chloroplast origin before they reach a taxonomic classifier.
    - Use Case: Removes a known source of misclassification in rRNA metabarcoding — organellar and cross-domain SSU co-amplified by the same universal primers — for any 16S/18S environmental or host-associated metagenomics pipeline, running ~3.4x faster with ~35% lower peak memory than Metaxa2 at the read scale (200,000 reads) a typical long-read amplicon run reaches.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, comprehensive README (including a candid discussion of a lower-confidence bacteria-vs-mitochondria boundary), 38 commits, 2 stars — brand new, single dependency-light binary (Bioconda or build from source); too early to assess issue-responsiveness.

💡 Methodological Trend

**First community challenge for automated virus taxonomy** (Lood, Doijad, Adriaenssens et al. — the ICTV Virus Bioinformatics and Expert Group, posted 2026-07-06, [GitHub](https://github.com/ictv-vbeg/ICTV-TaxonomyChallenge)) tackles focus area 10 from the infrastructure side rather than with a single new tool: 17 teams submitted 34 independent, fully reproducible pipelines for assigning viral contigs to all fifteen ICTV taxonomic ranks, benchmarked on a shared expert-curated-plus-metagenomic dataset. Most pipelines handled viruses already close to known species/genera/families well; viruses unclassified at those lower ranks remain the hard case — exactly the "dark matter" problem focus area 1 is about, and a reminder that automated virus discovery still needs a human-verified taxonomy backstop. It stays out of Section A because there is no single tool to adopt, but the datasets, benchmark harness and open-source pipelines are worth returning to the next time an unbiased metagenomic virus-discovery workflow needs a taxonomy-assignment step — and it is a template for the kind of open, head-to-head benchmarking **PathoBench** (Section A, above) is attempting for pathogen bioinformatics more broadly.

ℹ️ Section B: Informational & Proprietary

- **Bovine-derived H5N1 influenza virus efficiently infects lactating swine via the mammary gland** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.18.739312)) — experimental infection study, no software release [SURVEILLANCE-READY] #one-health #surveillance-ready
    - Function: Liu, Chillson, Martin, Cochran, Park, O'Boyle, Huey, Corps, Bowman, Warren (Ohio State University) intramammary-inoculated vaccinated lactating sows with bovine-derived H5N1 and co-housed them with piglets, finding sustained viral RNA shedding in milk, mammary lesions and neutralizing-antibody seroconversion — but no overt clinical disease — plus sporadic oral viral RNA positivity in piglets. H5N1 is an explicit NVI/EFSA priority pathogen; this is the first direct evidence that the mammary-gland tropism driving the US dairy-cattle outbreak can productively and *subclinically* infect commercial swine, a spillover route that would go undetected without expanded viral surveillance in swine, not just cattle.
- **Evolutionary trade-offs between antimicrobial resistance and virulence in *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.08.737271)) — experimental evolution study, no software release #amr #evolution
    - Function: Pal, Mohn, Habig, Pees, Schulenburg (University of Kiel) experimentally evolved *P. aeruginosa* under three antibiotics with distinct targets and combined resistance phenotyping, life-history traits, *in-vivo C. elegans* virulence assays and whole-genome sequencing to causally test the resistance-virulence trade-off, free of the confounded evolutionary histories of clinical isolates. Ciprofloxacin resistance correlated *positively* with virulence, piperacillin/tazobactam resistance correlated *negatively* (via a large deletion removing virulence genes), and streptomycin resistance barely changed virulence — challenging the assumption of a universal AMR-virulence trade-off that an AMR risk assessment might otherwise lean on.
- **Shifts in Genetic Diversity of Porcine Reproductive and Respiratory Syndrome Virus 2 in Vietnam Before and After African Swine Fever** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.06.736905)) — molecular epidemiology/phylodynamic analysis, no software release #one-health #evolution
    - Function: Nguyen, Pamornchainavakul, Herrera da Silva, Thanawongnuwech, VanderWaal (University of Minnesota et al.) integrate 366 Vietnamese PRRSV-2 ORF5 sequences (2007-2024) with a global lineage reference to show the ASF epidemic reshaped Vietnam's PRRSV-2 population from one dominant endemic sub-lineage to multiple co-circulating lineages, including two newly designated sub-lineages (L1M, L10B) repeatedly introduced from Thailand. A transboundary swine pathogen's lineage structure shifting as a side-effect of a different epidemic (ASF, an explicit NVI/EFSA priority pathogen) argues for continuous genomic surveillance of *co-circulating* swine pathogens during any major disease event, not just the one causing it.
- **One Health genomic surveillance reveals structured urban rabies transmission and major surveillance gaps** (bioRxiv preprint, Brunker, Davila-Barclay, Diaz, Kasaragod, Durrant, Hampson, Zegarra, Monroy, Castillo-Neyra, posted 2026-07-10) — genomic surveillance study, no software release #one-health
    - Function: Applying genomic surveillance to dog-mediated rabies in a dense urban setting (Arequipa, Peru), the authors find structured, spatially persistent local transmission foci rather than a single diffuse epidemic, with major gaps in what current surveillance actually captures — a directly reusable finding for any urban rabies-elimination programme, since micro-scale spatial structure needs resolving, not just city-wide case counts.
- **A chromosome-level reference genome of the largest cervid species — the European moose (*Alces alces*)** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.03.736352)) — reference genome assembly, no software release #assembly #comparative-genomics
    - Function: Torresen, Mysterud, Skage, Danneels, Strand, Ferrari, Tooming-Klunderud, Jakobsen (University of Oslo) produced a haplotype-resolved, chromosome-level genome assembly (two ~3.1 Gb pseudo-haplotypes, BUSCO 98.3%/95.7%, ~21,000 annotated genes each) for the European moose. A high-quality Norwegian cervid reference is useful groundwork for any future CWD (chronic wasting disease, an explicit NVI/EFSA priority pathogen) host-genomics work and for Scandinavian moose population/conservation genomics generally.
