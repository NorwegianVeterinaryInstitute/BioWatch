🚀 Section A: Command-Line & Secure Analytical Tools (Newest First)

- **PanVariants** (Release Date: 2026-04-22) #variant-calling #comparative-genomics #nextflow
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/MGI-EU/PanVariants) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.22.720142)
    - Function: Yi, Ni et al. (MGI/BGI, with DeepVariant co-author P.-C. Chang) built a pangenome-referenced, AI-assisted variant-calling pipeline that combines k-mer-based and mapping-based genotyping against a pangenome graph to call SNVs, indels (<50bp), CNVs and short tandem repeats, aiming to recover novel variants that graph-only callers miss because they are restricted to variants already stored in the graph.
    - Use Case: General-purpose short-read variant calling (benchmarked on human data, but the pangenome+hybrid-caller approach is directly transferable to any species with an available pangenome graph), reporting a 73% reduction in SNV errors vs BWA+GATK and 45% vs DRAGEN.
    - Usability Note: GPL-3.0, README and LICENSE present, Nextflow + Singularity container support (.sif via Zenodo), 13 commits, 10 stars, 1 open issue — too new to assess issue-responsiveness or ongoing maintenance.

- **Verticall** (Release Date: 2026-04-21) #comparative-genomics #evolution #python
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/rrwick/Verticall) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.21.719734)
    - Function: Odih, Wick, Holt (Holt lab) built a non-parametric tool that classifies genomic regions between genome pairs as vertically or horizontally inherited, from the distribution of pairwise genetic distances, producing recombination-filtered phylogenies that scale from clonal outbreak datasets to genus-wide diversity (validated on four public datasets spanning 154–4,857 genomes).
    - Use Case: Recombination detection/removal for phylogenomic analysis of bacterial pathogens at any evolutionary scale — directly relevant to within-species outbreak reconstruction and species/genus-level AMR-lineage tracking where existing recombination-detection tools (e.g. Gubbins) don't scale to large or genus-spanning datasets.
    - Usability Note: GPL-3.0, README present, 210 commits, 76 stars — mature, from an established outbreak/pangenomics lab (Kat Holt, Ryan Wick: Kleborate, Trycycler, Badread) with a strong maintenance track record; issue-responsiveness not individually checked this run.

- **DNAharvester** (Release Date: 2026-04-20) #assembly #metagenomics #nextflow
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/NBISweden/DNAharvester) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.20.719564)
    - Function: Sharif, Dalén et al. (NBIS, the Swedish national bioinformatics infrastructure) built a modular Nextflow pipeline for highly degraded ancient/historical DNA, combining metagenomic pre-filtering, competitive/adaptive-aligner mapping (BWA-aln/mem, Bowtie2), reference-bias evaluation, iterative mitogenome assembly, pathogen screening of unmapped reads, genetic sex determination and variant calling in one containerised workflow.
    - Use Case: End-to-end, reproducible processing of low-DNA-quantity/high-damage samples — the pathogen-screening subworkflow on unmapped reads is directly reusable for ancient/historical pathogen or zoonotic-reservoir detection in museum/archive specimens, beyond its core palaeogenomics use case.
    - Usability Note: GPL-3.0, README and LICENSE present, 1,285 commits, 14 stars, 14 open issues — a mature codebase from an established national infrastructure provider; issue volume too high to check response latency case-by-case this run.

- **TB-Bench** (Release Date: 2026-04-13) #amr #python

    - Function: VP, Narayanan et al. systematically benchmark 20 traditional-ML and deep-learning methods (drawn from 8 prior studies) for predicting second-line TB drug resistance from *M. tuberculosis* WGS data, standardising sequence encodings (including frequency chaos-game representation and SD-CNN) and evaluation across 14 drugs in one framework.
    - Use Case: A reusable benchmarking harness for anyone developing or evaluating a new AMR-prediction-from-sequence method for *M. tuberculosis* — directly relevant to focus area 2 (AMR phenotype prediction from sequence alone) and a template adaptable to other pathogens' drug-resistance-prediction benchmarks.
