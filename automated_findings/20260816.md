- **Protal** (Release Date: 2026-08-06) #metagenomics #variant-calling
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/4less/protal) | [Web](http://protal.earlham.ac.uk) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.03.742433)
    - Function: Fritscher, Duncan, Hildebrand (Quadram Institute Bioscience) built an alignment-based metagenome profiler that combines a new alignment algorithm, ML-based classification and conserved marker genes to profile all 143,614 bacterial/archaeal species in GTDB r226 at both species and strain level in a single pass.
    - Use Case: Beat MetaPhlAn 4, mOTUs4, sylph and Kraken2+Bracken on CAMI2 precision benchmarks (98.3% mean species-level precision) and showed the largest gains on rare single-genome-reference species and highly complex (10,000-species) communities — strain-level phylogenies come out of the same run up to 40x faster than running a dedicated strain-profiling workflow afterwards, making strain-resolved profiling of thousands of samples (e.g. longitudinal AMR/outbreak surveillance) feasible on commodity hardware.
    - Usability Note: GPL-2.0, comprehensive README (conda install), 267 commits, 16 stars — described by the authors as still "in final steps of development"; too early to assess issue-responsiveness but an actively developed repository.

- **ASTRAL-X** (Release Date: 2026-08-06) #phylogenetics #comparative-genomics
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/aaniksahaa/ASTRAL-X) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.31.742122)
    - Function: Saha & Bayzid (Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology) completely redesigned the widely-used coalescent-based species-tree estimator ASTRAL's data representations and algorithms to remove its computational/memory bottlenecks at ultra-large scale.
    - Use Case: Reconstructs statistically consistent species trees for 200,000 and 300,000 taxa in 5 and 12 hours respectively on modest hardware (vs. being computationally impractical for ASTRAL at this scale), while preserving ASTRAL's statistical guarantees and matching its accuracy — directly reusable for any large-scale phylogenomic/Tree-of-Life effort (e.g. tree-to-tree comparison across thousands of pathogen or host genomes) that previously had to subsample taxa to stay tractable.
    - Usability Note: Comprehensive README (self-contained platform binaries bundling their own Java runtime, plus a developer build path), 258 commits, 2 stars, already at v1.1.0 with a documented bug-fix changelog — active development; license not explicitly stated in the repository, worth confirming before production use.

The **AI-biosecurity-screening thread** (DNAS-Bench, [[20260802]]) gained two more entries: **Pervasive Backdoor Vulnerabilities in Genomic Foundation Models** (Ni, Wang, Wei et al. — Shenzhen Institutes of Advanced Technology, CAS, posted 2026-08-04) shows that poisoning as little as 5% of fine-tuning data with short DNA motifs (including transposon terminal-inverted-repeat-derived triggers) reliably backdoors genomic foundation models across three model families, seven parameter scales (50M-7B) and 18 classification tasks, with attack success rates of 91-100% and no protective effect from scaling up model size — a direct argument for data-provenance and post-training security audits before trusting a fine-tuned genomic FM in a surveillance pipeline; no code repository for this specific paper was found, though an unrelated but topically adjacent prior paper ("Poisoning the Genome," Georgakopoulos-Soares lab) has published code. **Safety First: Input Screening for Protein Design Tools** (Palmer, Teran, Wheeler, Yassif — Nuclear Threat Initiative, posted 2026-08-07) proposes screening AI protein-binder-design tools by the *target* they are asked to bind (not the designed binder itself), using ESM-C protein-language-model embeddings to catch functionally dangerous targets that sequence-similarity screening (BLASTP-style) misses — flagging 20-23% of mammalian targets but under 1.1% of bacterial/fungal/plant/viral ones, with no public code/database release found this run. Separately, the **agentic-AI-for-scientific-computing thread** (BacteReason/biomeStat/ChatGEM/MetaClaw in earlier cycles) produced an unusually substantive field report rather than a single tool: **Scientific computing in the age of agentic AI** (Li, Rubinsteyn, Feldman, O'Donnell, Ferguson, Patro, Ewels, Krueger, Pedersen et al. — OpenAI with maintainers from salmon/nf-core/scanpy and other widely-used bioinformatics tools, posted 2026-08-04) reports eight case studies of LLM coding agents applied directly to maintaining and rewriting real, in-production scientific software (not toy benchmarks), concluding the approach genuinely helps with the technical-debt and engineering-labour shortage endemic to small-team scientific tooling, but flags open questions around ownership/responsibility for agent-driven changes to community infrastructure — worth watching as a signal that the maintainers of tools BioWatch already tracks (nf-core workflows, etc.) are starting to use agents on their own codebases.

- **It happened again - emergence of a Shamonda-like orthobunyavirus in Central Europe, 2026** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.06.743243)) — outbreak/genomic characterisation, no software release #one-health #emerging
    - Function: Wernike, Hoffmann, Link, Rotheneder, Eshak, Pfaff, Hoeper, Beer (Friedrich-Loeffler-Institut) trace an acute, high-morbidity dairy-cattle syndrome (reduced milk yield, diarrhea, fever) across southern Germany and Switzerland since mid-2026 to a previously undescribed "Shamonda-like" Simbu-serogroup orthobunyavirus, recovered as a complete genome by metagenomic sequencing after a pan-Simbuvirus PCR screen. More than a decade after Schmallenberg virus's 2011 emergence in the same serogroup and region, a second novel livestock arbovirus appearing in Central Europe is a direct cross-border cattle-health watch-list candidate, relevant to any Nordic/European livestock surveillance programme watching for the same vector-borne route.
- **R-pyocin-mediated selection reverses pan-drug resistance in Pseudomonas aeruginosa** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.04.742823)) — experimental evolution study, no software release #amr
    - Function: Estrada, Campbell, Welch, Smith, Goldberg, Rumbaugh, Diggle (Georgia Institute of Technology) show that sustained R-pyocin (phage-like bactericidal particle) pressure on pan-drug-resistant *P. aeruginosa* selects for large chromosomal deletions that knock out the LPS receptor (R-pyocin resistance) but also delete the *mexXY* efflux operon and multiple virulence/biofilm genes as collateral damage — restoring aminoglycoside susceptibility and attenuating virulence in a wound-infection model. A genuinely novel "evolutionary trap" strategy for this WHO critical-priority pathogen: steering resistance evolution itself toward a clinically manageable phenotype rather than just killing cells outright.
- **Parallel Emergence and Adaptive Evolution of Multicountry Circulating Mycobacterium abscessus Clones** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.02.742357)) — comparative genomics/population study, no software release #amr #evolution
    - Function: Zhu, Gan, Zhou, Eick, Solomon, Yang, Friedland, Gross, Miller, Olivier, Zhu, Li, Liu (University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill et al.) analyse 11,314 public *M. abscessus* genomes from 30 countries and identify 38 recently-emerged, multicountry-circulating clones (beyond the seven previously known Dominant Circulating Clones), with clone-level variation in macrolide-susceptibility genotype and 55 genes showing a shift from purifying to positive selection during recent clonal expansion. A reminder that this recently-emerged opportunistic pathogen's global spread is driven by an actively growing set of distinct lineages, not a handful of static clones — relevant to any AMR genomic-surveillance programme that currently only screens against the seven named DCCs.
- **A One-Shot Multivalent Live-Attenuated Candidate Influenza Vaccine against Divergent Zoonotic H5N1 Clades** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.02.742385)) — vaccine development study, no software release [SURVEILLANCE-READY] #one-health
    - Function: Elsayed, Barre, Rahmani, Escobedo, Jeevan, Nath, Castro, Shivanna, Nogales, Kobie, Webby, Abdelwhab, Garcia-Sastre, Martinez-Sobrido (Texas Biomedical Research Institute et al.) build a single live-attenuated influenza vaccine carrying HA/NA from three divergent H5N1 clades (2.3.4.4b, 2.3.2.1a, 2.3.2.1e) on a cold-adapted master donor backbone, showing one intranasal dose gives mice broadly cross-reactive antibodies and full protection against homologous challenge. A pandemic-preparedness-relevant proof of concept for covering multiple co-circulating H5N1 lineages (an explicit NVI/EFSA priority pathogen) with a single vaccine dose rather than clade-matched boosters.
