🚀 Section A: Command-Line & Secure Analytical Tools (Newest First)

- **Fung-AI** (Release Date: 2026-03-10) #amr #one-health #emerging
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/jhuapl-bio/fungai) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.09.710548) | [PLOS Computational Biology](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014105)
    - Function: Berman, Dunphy et al. (Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory, with Casadevall) built an AI/ML pipeline that trains a GAN to generate novel candidate antifungal peptide sequences, then prioritizes ~10,000 candidates with in-silico antifungal- and hemolytic-activity classifiers before wet-lab testing.
    - Use Case: De novo antifungal peptide design for the wheat pathogen *Fusarium graminearum* and the human/animal pathogen *Candida albicans* — directly relevant to focus area 9 (food security/AMR beyond antibiotics) given rising fungicide and antifungal resistance; five of thirteen synthesized peptides showed mild antifungal activity, with two also showing low mammalian-cell cytotoxicity as scaffolds for further optimization.
    - Usability Note: Apache-2.0 license, README present, but only 1 commit (too new to assess issue-responsiveness or ongoing maintenance) — already peer-reviewed and published in *PLOS Computational Biology* on the same posting, a stronger-than-average maturity signal for a brand-new repository.

- **DartUniFrac** (Release Date: 2026-03-03) #metagenomics #emerging
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/jianshu93/DartUniFrac) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.01.708916)
    - Function: Zhao, Knight et al. (UC San Diego) connect UniFrac to weighted Jaccard similarity and exploit weighted MinHash sketching for a GPU-accelerated UniFrac implementation up to three orders of magnitude faster than the state of the art, scaling to millions of samples and billions of taxa while remaining statistically indistinguishable from exact UniFrac on real microbiome/metagenomic datasets.
    - Use Case: Makes phylogeny-aware beta-diversity computation tractable at megascale cohort/surveillance size — directly relevant to any large wastewater, environmental or multi-site metagenomic surveillance pipeline currently bottlenecked by exact UniFrac's quadratic cost.
    - Usability Note: BSD-3-Clause license, comprehensive README (installation, usage, benchmarks), Rust implementation with CPU/GPU paths, 212 commits, 1 open issue and 1 open PR — active development from an established Knight-lab UniFrac-tooling lineage.

- **selscape** (Release Date: 2026-03-03) #evolution #snakemake
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/xin-huang/selscape) | [Docs](https://xin-huang.github.io/selscape) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.02.28.708717)
    - Function: Chen & Huang (University of Vienna) built a Snakemake workflow that automates end-to-end genome-wide natural-selection analysis — input preparation, running multiple selection statistics (e.g. betascan for balancing selection, selscan for positive selection, dadi-cli for the distribution of fitness effects), functional annotation and summary reporting — across arbitrary population sets.
    - Use Case: A reproducible, scalable alternative to hand-chaining separate selection-scan tools, directly applicable to focus area 5 ("selection analysis") for any population genomic dataset, including pathogen or host-adaptation studies, not just the human 1000 Genomes demonstration in the preprint.
    - Usability Note: GPL-3.0 license, README present, CI-tested documentation site, 51 commits, 3 open issues — modest adoption so far (1 star) but an actively developed, well-documented workflow for a newly posted tool.

- **HMM-Flagger** (Release Date: 2026-03-02) #assembly
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/mobinasri/flagger) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.02.27.708355)
    - Function: Asri, Paten et al. (UC Santa Cruz Genomics Institute, with Eichler/Miga) present a reference-free tool that models read coverage over a haplotype-resolved genome assembly with a hidden Markov model augmented by a Gaussian autoregressive process, classifying coverage anomalies as erroneous, falsely duplicated, or collapsed blocks — superseding the authors' earlier Flagger v0.4.0.
    - Use Case: Reference-free structural-error detection for difficult/repetitive diploid or haplotype-resolved genome assemblies (focus area 4, high priority) — detected synthetic misassemblies at F1=78.4% (PacBio HiFi) and 60.4% (ONT R10), and quantified a 0.94%→0.38% assembly error-rate improvement between HPRC releases 1 and 2.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, comprehensive README with WDL workflows and per-platform presets, 1,258 commits — a mature, very actively developed tool; 19 open issues is enough volume that this run could not confirm maintainer-response latency case by case, but there is no indication of the "commits but no replies" pattern flagged in earlier cycles.

💡 Methodological Trend

The dual-use AI/biosecurity thread tracked last cycle ([[20260301]]: AI-assisted customer verification for synthetic nucleic acid screening) continued this cycle from the other side of the same problem. **The Limits of Sequence-Based Biosecurity Screening Tools in the Age of AI-Assisted Protein Design** (Wittmann, Horvitz et al., Microsoft, posted 2026-03-05, now published in *Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology*) stress-tested four biosecurity screening (BSS) tools against AI-reformulated synthetic homologs of proteins of concern fragmented into short segments, finding two of four tools already robust down to 50-nucleotide fragments (exceeding the US Framework for Nucleic Acid Synthesis' requirements) and the other two improved after patching — a useful, if unsettling, benchmark of how screening infrastructure is holding up against generative protein design, rather than a new tool itself. Separately, a strong instance of focus-area-10 novelty with no corresponding software: **Selection-free whole genome transplantation revives dead microbes** (Seidel, Venter, Glass et al., J. Craig Venter Institute, posted 2026-03-14) built the first living synthetic bacterial cell from non-living parts — killing *Mycoplasma capricolum* cells by crosslinking their genome, then reviving them by installing a synthetic *Mycoplasma mycoides* genome without relying on an antibiotic-resistance selection marker. This removes a long-standing barrier (false-positive transplants from donor-marker recombination) to extending whole-genome transplantation beyond the one Mollicutes clade it has worked in so far — worth rechecking in future cycles as the technique is generalized to other bacterial taxa. A third candidate, **APEX** (Attention-based Protein EXplainer; Polonio et al., University of Málaga, posted 2026-03-02), combines ESM-2 embeddings with an explainable graph-attention/GNNExplainer pipeline to prioritize antimicrobial and antifungal drug targets and guide inhibitor design — a focus-area-2 pLM application directly aimed at fungicide-resistance and antimicrobial target discovery — but no public code repository could be confirmed this run, so it is flagged here rather than in Section A pending a code release.
