🚀 Section A: Command-Line & Secure Analytical Tools (Newest First)

- **DORAS (Dynamically Optimized Reference Adaptive Sampling)** (Release Date: 2026-05-29) [SURVEILLANCE-READY] #amr #variant-calling #python
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/RametteLab/DORAS) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.26.727915)
    - Function: Borcard, Gempeler, Terrazos Miani, Casanova, Ramette (Institute for Infectious Diseases, University of Bern) built a PCR-free Python tool that dynamically extends the Oxford Nanopore adaptive-sampling reference during a live sequencing run, overcoming the poor read-recruitment of naive ReadUntil-based MLST targeting.
    - Use Case: Full MLST profiles for outbreak/clinical isolates in about half the time of a 30X WGS run (3 h vs 6 h), with no extra hands-on library prep — validated on hospital-outbreak *Corynebacterium diphtheriae*, vancomycin-resistant enterococci, and routine clinical *E. coli* — a direct fit for any lab running Nanopore-based MLST surveillance or outbreak typing on a budget.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, README present, 21 commits, 0 stars — brand new from an established clinical-microbiology genomics group; Pixi-based install (conda "coming soon"), too young to assess issue-responsiveness.

- **savont** (Release Date: 2026-05-29) #metagenomics #variant-calling
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/bluenote-1577/savont) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.26.727271)
    - Function: Shaw, Riisgaard-Jensen, Andersen, Kirkegaard, Dueholm, Li (Dana-Farber/Harvard plus the Dueholm long-read-microbiome lab) built a Rust tool that recovers amplicon sequence variants (ASVs) at single-nucleotide resolution from noisy long reads (ONT R10.4, PacBio HiFi) at ≥98% mean accuracy.
    - Use Case: Needs 5-16x lower sequencing depth than prior long-read ASV methods and recovers up to 95x more ASVs in complex communities — makes full-length 16S (or other marker-gene) ASV-level profiling practical on shallow, cheap long-read runs, directly reusable for wastewater/environmental or gut-microbiome surveillance pipelines.
    - Usability Note: MIT license, comprehensive README, 110 commits, 72 stars — from Heng Li's group plus the established Dueholm long-read amplicon lab; mature and actively developed despite the preprint being new this cycle. Cargo or bioconda install.

- **gTranslate** (Release Date: 2026-05-28) #annotation #comparative-genomics #python
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/cmc-aau/gTranslate) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.24.727570) | [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/gtdb-gtranslate/)
    - Function: Chaumeil, Hugenholtz, Parks (the GTDB/GTDB-Tk team) built an ensemble-of-five-ML-models tool that predicts which genetic translation table a prokaryotic genome uses from gene-coding-density and UGA-reassignment features, without needing a prior taxonomic classification.
    - Use Case: Removes a manual, easy-to-get-wrong step (specifying translation table 4/11/25) from any gene-calling/annotation pipeline run ahead of taxonomic placement — >99.99% correct (less than 1 error per 10,000 genomes), and used to find the first UGA-to-tryptophan reassignment in the phylum Patescibacteriota.
    - Usability Note: GPL-3.0, comprehensive README, 56 commits, 7 stars, pip (`gtdb-gtranslate`) and bioconda installable — from the same lab maintaining GTDB-Tk, a strong existing maintenance track record.

- **Tandem** (Release Date: 2026-05-26) #amr #comparative-genomics #python
    - Links: [GitHub](https://github.com/yuingan/tandem) | [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.22.727201)
    - Function: Ngan & Smith (University of Cambridge) built a three-module Python tool that detects bacterial tandem gene duplications by NUCmer self-alignment, classifies each by homologous-recombination signature and junction microhomology, and quantifies known junctions in population sequencing via a novel Junction Read Ratio metric — filling a gap left by duplication-agnostic callers like breseq.
    - Use Case: Tandem duplication is a known route to antibiotic resistance, metabolic adaptation and gene-family expansion in bacteria; Tandem gives a reusable way to detect, mechanistically classify, and track the frequency of such duplications through an evolving or outbreak population (100% recall, 4.3% mean absolute error on 280 artificial population tests across seven species).
    - Usability Note: MIT license, README and test data present, 20 commits, 0 stars — brand new academic release, too young to assess issue-responsiveness. Requires NUCmer (MUMmer4), minimap2, samtools via conda.

💡 Methodological Trend

Two unrelated releases this cycle show genomic foundation models scaling into new territory (focus area 10). **Carbon** (Allal, von Werra et al., Hugging Face, posted 2026-05-25, [code/weights released](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.22.727119)) is a 3B/8B-parameter open generative DNA language model using 6-mer tokenization to reach a ~786 kbp context window, competitive with Evo2-7B at under half the parameters — notable less for the benchmark numbers than for who built it: a major AI-tooling company (makers of `transformers`/SmolLM) treating genomic sequence modelling as mainstream infrastructure rather than a niche academic exercise. In the same vein but domain-specific, **Genos-m** (Fang, Zhong et al., BGI-HangzhouAI, posted 2026-05-24, [GitHub](https://github.com/BGI-HangzhouAI/Genos-m)) is a 4.7B-parameter Mixture-of-Experts foundation model pretrained specifically on human-associated microbial genomes (isolates, MAGs, phages) with a 1 Mbp context, generating stable sample-level embeddings from as few as 10,000 metagenomic reads that preserve community structure — a plausible future building block for low-depth AMR/microbiome surveillance once (if) retrained or fine-tuned on veterinary/environmental rather than human-gut corpora. Neither tool is veterinary- or AMR-specific yet, but both are fully open (code and weights on GitHub/Hugging Face), making them worth rechecking if a livestock- or wastewater-focused fine-tune appears.

- **Breeding pig transport drives the dispersal of swine influenza A virus across Europe** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.01.729471)) — phylogeographic/epidemiological analysis, no software release #one-health #evolution
    - Function: Meester, Vrancken, Gambaro, Dellicour (Utrecht University et al.) reconstruct European swine-IAV spread and find that breeding-pig trade — not pork trade or geographic distance — is the only consistently supported predictor of cross-border viral transitions, flagging breeding-pig-importing farms as reassortment hotspots and a zoonotic-spillover risk point.
- **Local Temperature and Humidity are Associated with Proportion of Antimicrobial-Resistant Escherichia coli isolates in Farm Environments** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.27.728203)) — on-farm AMR surveillance modelling, no software release #amr #one-health
    - Function: Vass, Stanton, Avison, Reyher, Dowsey et al. (University of Bristol) fit a non-linear Bayesian model (correcting for abundance-dependent test sensitivity) to 2,766 dairy-farm *E. coli* samples and find warmer, wetter conditions are associated with higher proportions of streptomycin-, tetracycline- and amoxicillin-resistant isolates — a concrete argument for building weather/seasonality terms into on-farm AMR surveillance designs ahead of climate change.
- **Environmental Microbial Community Signatures Associated with Listeria spp. Detection in German Meat Processing Facilities** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.25.727608)) — year-long environmental/MLST survey, no software release #one-health
    - Function: Braun, Wildi, Kovac, Guldimann (LMU Munich) combine 16S amplicon sequencing, culture and MLST across six German meat plants over a year and show floor drains are the key ecological niche for *Listeria monocytogenes* persistence, with *Acinetobacter*, *Rhizorhapis* and *Vagococcus* ASVs positively associated with Listeria-positive drains — a direct lead for food-safety programmes tracking this NVI/EFSA priority pathogen to look at resident drain microbiota, not just Listeria presence/absence.
- **Two epidemics, one genotype, different outcomes: evolutionary changes of Avian Influenza H5N1, genotype EA-2024-DI** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.25.727580)) — large multi-country phylodynamic analysis, no software release [SURVEILLANCE-READY] #one-health #evolution #surveillance-ready
    - Function: Zecchin, Monne, Fusaro et al. (EU Reference Laboratory for Avian Influenza, IZS delle Venezie, plus national labs across ~20 European countries) track the EA-2024-DI H5N1 genotype across three epidemic waves (2023-2026), showing it has persisted and evolved, with a shift in diffusion epicentre from Eastern to Central Europe and a recent rise in mammalian-adaptive mutations — a direct input for any European or Norwegian H5N1 risk assessment.
- **Antimicrobial use contributes to resistance gene enrichment across cattle groups on commercial dairy farms** ([bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.22.726633)) — farm-level AMU/resistome metagenomic study, no software release #amr #one-health
    - Function: Steinberger, Hite et al. (University of Wisconsin-Madison) pair detailed farm AMU records with bovine fecal metagenomics across calves, lactating, sick and cull cows, finding that tetracycline resistance is ubiquitous regardless of current AMU while aminoglycoside/β-lactam/macrolide resistance tracks AMU specifically, and that calves carry the highest resistance-gene richness — a concrete argument for stratifying AMR surveillance by host group, not just by farm.
